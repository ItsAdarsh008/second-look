Nobody who approved Starbucks Korea's "Tank Day" was trying to cause offence.

A tumbler size called the Tank. A promotion on May 18. The slogan "Thwack it on the table!" Each piece was harmless. Together, in Korea, they evoked the tanks sent into Gwangju on May 18, 1980, and the 1987 police claim that a student died under torture after officers "hit the desk with a thwack." The campaign was pulled within hours and the CEO was fired.

Most review tools would have checked the image. The image was fine.

Second Look reviews the whole campaign: creative, product name, headline, body copy, markets, launch date and channel. It checks each on its own and then in combination, against a corpus of documented failures and each market's memorial and religious calendar. Every finding names the exact element and the exact referent, and cites where the risk comes from. When there's nothing to find, it says so, and says that's not an approval.

For findings that live in the picture, it uses the Magic Hour API to render an alternative for the team to react to in the meeting. It's labeled as an alternative to consider, with the report still attached.

It's a first read, not a substitute for someone who lives in the market. It's there so that person gets asked the right question in time.
